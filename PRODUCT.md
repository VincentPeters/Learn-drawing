# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primarily Vincent — a self-learner working through a 45-lesson technical-drawing course at a drawing desk, with the site open on a laptop or tablet *next to* paper, pencil in hand. The dominant context is mid-session: glancing between screen and drawing board, following timed warm-up / main / checkpoint blocks, marking lessons complete.

Secondary: anyone he shares the link with. The site must hold up publicly — a stranger should land, understand the course in one screen, and be able to start Lesson 1. Portfolio-adjacent quality, but it is a working tool first, not a pitch.

## Product Purpose

A free, self-contained companion website for learning general technical and engineering drawing: 45 Markdown lessons in two modules (Technical Drawing; Rendering & Colour), hand-authored SVG diagrams, a visual reference/cheat-sheet, and local-only progress. Fully offline-capable, zero external requests.

Success means *sustained practice*: the learner comes back day after day, finds their place instantly ("continue where you left off"), gets through a session without friction, and finishes the course. Every design decision serves the returning practitioner over the first-time browser.

## Brand Personality

Warm studio character — crafted, calm, credible. The feel of a well-worn field notebook crossed with an old technical manual: honest materials, confident dense figures, stamped/printed type, a human workshop-teacher voice. Personal and a bit analog, never cute about it.

## Anti-references

- **SaaS course platforms** (Udemy/Coursera energy): no gamification chrome, badges, streaks-as-spectacle, or marketing gradients.
- **The blueprint cliché**: the previous design's move — blueprint blue everywhere, grid-paper background, drafting-theme-as-costume. Banned.
- **Cozy stationery aesthetic**: no faux paper textures, washi-tape skeuomorphism, or hand-drawn-font cuteness.
- **Sterile docs site**: it must not read as developer documentation — no docs-theme austerity.

## Design Principles

1. **The lesson is the loudest thing.** During a session, chrome recedes; the content and its timed blocks carry the visual weight.
2. **Drawn along with, not just read.** Design for the glance-back: the learner looks away to draw and looks back — current position, timers, and steps must be re-findable at arm's length in one glance.
3. **Craft is the credibility.** A site teaching precise line work must itself be precisely set: real typographic hierarchy, measured spacing, deliberate rules and line weights. The draftsmanship is earned in the typesetting, not applied as a theme.
4. **Print-honest materials.** Solid inks, ruled lines, figures with captions — like a good printed manual. No glass, no glow, no decorative blur.
5. **Progress without gamification.** Completion is a quiet, factual mark — a checked line in an index — never confetti.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: ≥4.5:1 body-text contrast, semantic markup, full keyboard support (including the existing ←/→ lesson navigation), visible focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all animation. Progress stays in localStorage; nothing is uploaded.
