Quick reference

The technical drawing cheat sheet

Both modules, condensed into diagrams you can glance at mid-drawing. Drafting conventions first, then the rendering and colour of Module 2. Bookmark this page.

The alphabet of lines

Every line on a drawing means something specific, set by its style and weight. Learn to read and draw all seven.

Orthographic projection

The core of engineering drawing: unfold the "glass box" so a 3-D part becomes aligned 2-D views. Front, top, and right are usually enough.

First angle vs third angle

Two conventions for where the views land. The symbol in the title block tells the reader which one you used.

Pictorial views

Show depth on flat paper. Isometric keeps true lengths on three axes; the isometric circle is a leaning ellipse.

Dimensioning & sections

Turn a shape into a spec. Dimension every feature once; cut the part open to show what's inside and hatch the solid material.

Auxiliary views & threads

Look straight at a slanted face to get its true shape; use standard shorthand for threads and tapped holes.

Intersections & developments

Plot the curve where two solids meet, then unfold a surface into the flat pattern a shop would cut.

Assemblies & the sheet

Show how parts fit with balloons and a BOM, then package it on a bordered sheet with a proper title block.

Value & hatching MODULE 2

Turn line into tone. Build an even value scale, and let the stroke direction describe the surface it sits on.

Light & shadow MODULE 2

One light source gives every solid the same six readable zones, and casts a shadow you can construct by projection.

Colour & finish MODULE 2

Graphite carries the structure; blue and sanguine carry temperature and focus. Edges and texture do the finishing.

Glossary

The terms you'll meet across both modules.

Orthographic projection
Representing an object with parallel projectors perpendicular to the picture plane, giving true-shape views.
First / third angle
Two ISO conventions for arranging projected views; indicated by a truncated-cone symbol.
Isometric
A pictorial with axes at 120° apart where all three principal directions share one scale.
Oblique
A pictorial keeping one face true-shape with depth receding at an angle (cavalier = full, cabinet = half).
Hidden line
A dashed line showing an edge concealed behind material.
Center line
A thin long-short line marking axes of symmetry and hole centers.
Section view
A view of the part as if cut by a plane, with cut solid material hatched.
Cutting-plane line
The heavy line showing where a section is taken, with arrows for the viewing direction.
Auxiliary view
A view projected onto a plane parallel to an inclined surface to show its true shape.
Dimension line
A thin line with arrowheads carrying a measurement between extension lines.
Tolerance
The total permissible variation of a dimension.
Fit
The relationship (clearance, transition, interference) between mating parts.
GD&T
Geometric Dimensioning & Tolerancing — a symbolic system for form, orientation, and position.
Intersection
The curve produced where the surfaces of two solids meet.
Development
The unfolded, flat pattern of a 3-D surface used for fabrication.
Title block
The boxed area holding title, scale, material, author, and projection symbol.
Scale
The ratio of drawn size to real size (e.g. 1:2); dimensions always state the real value.
BOM
Bill of materials — the itemized parts list keyed to assembly balloons.
Value
The lightness or darkness of a tone, independent of colour.
Hatching
Parallel strokes used to build tone; their direction can describe the surface.
Cross-hatching
Layers of hatching set at differing angles to deepen tone.
Terminator
The line on a form where light turns into shadow.
Core shadow
The darkest band of a form's own shadow, just inside the terminator.
Reflected light
Light bouncing back into the shadow, lifting the far rim of a form.
Cast shadow
The shadow an object throws onto another surface.
Occlusion
The darkest darks where two surfaces nearly meet.
Sanguine
A warm red-brown drawing pencil, traditional in academic study.
Temperature
The warmth or coolness of a tone; warm advances, cool recedes.
