By the end

What you'll actually be able to do

This is general engineering drawing, the kind used to describe real parts and machines. You'll learn it by hand rather than in CAD, because working it out on paper is what makes it stick.

Draw a confident line

Steady freehand lines and clean ellipses, plus the standard "alphabet of lines" that every drawing is built from.

Project any object

Turn a 3-D part into aligned front, top, and side views in both first and third angle, and work out a missing view when you're only given two.

Draw in 3-D

Isometric, oblique, and one- and two-point perspective, including the isometric circles and cylinders that trip most people up.

Dimension a part

Dimensioning, tolerances and fits, a first look at GD&T, and the shorthand for threads and fasteners.

Show the inside

Section views to reveal interior detail, and auxiliary views to find the true shape of a slanted surface.

Finish a real drawing

Intersections, flat-pattern developments, assemblies with a parts list, and a complete title-blocked capstone.

How the month is laid out

Five phases, in order

Each phase builds on the one before it, so it's worth doing them in order. The line control you pick up in the first week is what makes the harder geometry later feel manageable.

Before you start

Your kit and daily routine

You don't need much, and for learning the basics cheap pencil-and-paper tools genuinely beat any software. Here's the whole list.

Tools

  • PaperA3, smooth, 90–120 gsm. A grid or dot pad helps early on.
  • Pencils2H, HB, 2B. 2H for construction, HB/2B for outlines and tone.
  • AccentsOptional blue & a warm pencil for highlighting features.
  • EraserKneaded eraser plus a fine click eraser for details.
  • RulerFor checking proportions only — never for drawing lines.

Daily routine · 45–90 min

  • 10 minWarm-up. Freehand straight lines and ellipses.
  • 30–60 minMain exercise for the current day.
  • 10 minHatching / lettering practice on scrap.
  • RhythmSix short sessions a week beat one long weekend.
If you take one thing from this page, take this: show up often, even when you only have twenty minutes. Six short sessions a week will get you further than one long weekend push. Your hand learns this by repetition, not by cramming.
